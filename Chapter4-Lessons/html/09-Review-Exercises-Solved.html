Ex 1 · Burgers and pizza

A study shows that about 37 percent of school children eat burger, 17 percent eat pizza, and 10 percent eat both burger and pizza. What is the probability that a randomly selected school child eats neither burger nor pizza?

  1. Extract: P(B) = 0.37, P(Z) = 0.17, P(B ∩ Z) = 0.10.
  2. "Neither…nor" is the complement of the union. First get the union — they overlap, so Rule 2:
    P(B ∪ Z) = 0.37 + 0.17 − 0.10 = 0.44
  3. P(neither) = 1 − P(B ∪ Z) = 1 − 0.44 = 0.56

Cross-check with a Venn diagram: burger only = 0.37 − 0.10 = 0.27; pizza only = 0.17 − 0.10 = 0.07; both = 0.10; outside = 1 − (0.27 + 0.07 + 0.10) = 0.56. ✔

Ex 2 · Smart phone colors

If the probabilities are, respectively, 0.23, 0.15, 0.21, and 0.09 that a person purchasing a new smart phone will choose the color gold, silver, black, or white, what is the probability that a given buyer will purchase a new phone that comes in one of those colors?

  1. A phone has exactly one color, so the four events are mutually exclusive → extended Addition Rule 1.
  2. P(gold ∪ silver ∪ black ∪ white) = 0.23 + 0.15 + 0.21 + 0.09 = 0.68

Read what this tells you: the total is 0.68, not 1 — so 32% of buyers choose some other color entirely. A question that follows up with "find the probability the buyer chooses a different color" is answered by 1 − 0.68 = 0.32.

Ex 3 · Set algebra with probabilities

If P(A) = 0.3, P(B) = 0.2, and P(A ∩ B) = 0.1, determine: (a) P(Ac)  (b) P(A ∪ B)  (c) P(Ac ∩ B)  (d) P(A ∩ Bc)  (e) P((A ∪ B)c)  (f) P(Ac ∪ B)

Build the four-region picture first. A Venn diagram with two overlapping circles has exactly four regions, and every part of this question reads straight off it:

RegionMeaningProbability
A onlyA ∩ Bc0.3 − 0.1 = 0.2
BothA ∩ B0.1
B onlyAc ∩ B0.2 − 0.1 = 0.1
Neither(A ∪ B)c1 − 0.4 = 0.6
TotalS1.0 ✔
  1. (a) P(Ac) = 1 − P(A) = 1 − 0.3 = 0.7
  2. (b) P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B) = 0.3 + 0.2 − 0.1 = 0.4
  3. (c) P(Ac ∩ B) = "B but not A" = P(B) − P(A ∩ B) = 0.2 − 0.1 = 0.1
  4. (d) P(A ∩ Bc) = "A but not B" = P(A) − P(A ∩ B) = 0.3 − 0.1 = 0.2
  5. (e) P((A ∪ B)c) = 1 − P(A ∪ B) = 1 − 0.4 = 0.6
  6. (f) P(Ac ∪ B) = P(Ac) + P(B) − P(Ac ∩ B) = 0.7 + 0.2 − 0.1 = 0.8
    Or from the diagram: everything except "A only" = 1 − 0.2 = 0.8 ✔
The technique that makes this question trivial

Never attack set-algebra questions with formulas alone. Draw the two circles, fill in the four regions as numbers, and every sub-part becomes an addition of regions you can see. Parts (c), (d) and (f) take longer to write than to solve once the diagram exists.
