Chapter 2 organised data into frequency distributions and graphs. Chapter 3 summarises data numerically with three families of measures: central tendency (mean, median, mode, midrange, weighted mean), variation (range, variance, standard deviation) and position (z-scores, percentiles, quartiles, deciles, interquartile range). Together these make up traditional statistics. Hadhrami

0 · Parameter vs statistic, and the rounding rule Both · CLO1-A

A parameter is a numerical value that describes an entire population, computed from all its data values (e.g. μ, σ²). A statistic is a descriptive measure computed from a sample (e.g. x̄, s²). Statisticians usually study populations through samples. When a population is small, no sample is needed: the whole population can be studied.

General rounding rule

Do not round during the calculation, only at the final answer. Carry three or four decimal places through the working. Example 3-1 gives 578/9 ≈ 64.2, but the slide marks 64.222 as the better working value.

1 · The mean Both

A measure of central tendency summarises a whole data set with one value that represents its middle or centre. The mean (arithmetic average) is the sum of the values divided by the number of values. It applies to discrete and continuous data.

Sample mean (a statistic) · population mean (a parameter)

x̄ = X₁ + X₂ + ⋯ + Xₙn = ΣXn      μ = X₁ + X₂ + ⋯ + XNN = ΣXN

x̄ is pronounced "X bar", μ "mew". n = number of values in the sample, N = number of values in the population.

Ex 3-1 · Avian flu cases Both

Flu cases over 9 years: 4, 46, 98, 115, 88, 44, 73, 48, 62. Find the mean.

x̄ = 578/9 = 64.222 ≈ 64.2

The mean number of flu cases over the 9 years is 64.2.

Ex 3-2 · Store sales Hadhrami

Sales (in $ millions) over 5 years: 221, 239, 262, 281, 318. Find the mean.

x̄ = 1321/5 = 264.2

The mean is $264.2 million, which is not one of the data values.

Properties of the mean · memorise all six Both

(1) It uses all the data values. (2) It varies less than the median or mode across samples from the same population. (3) It is used to compute other statistics, such as the variance. (4) It is unique and usually not one of the data values. (5) It cannot be computed for a frequency distribution with an open-ended class. (6) It is strongly affected by outliers (extremely high or low values), so it may be the wrong average to report. Also, knowing the mean alone is not enough to describe a data set; see 3-2.

3 · The median (MD) Both

The median is the middle value of data that have been arranged in order. Steps: (1) order the data from smallest to largest; (2) count n; (3) if n is odd, the median is the middle value at position (n + 1)/2; if n is even, it is the mean of the values at positions n/2 and n/2 + 1.

Interpreting the median Hadhrami

If the median exam score in a section is 76, then 50% of students scored 76 or less and 50% scored 76 or more. The median is less sensitive to outliers and skewed data than the mean. Both

Activity · median Amani

Find the median of (a) 114, 77, 21, 101, 311, 77, 159, 382 and (b) 6180, 4894, 2821, 2559, 2483.

(a) Ordered: 21, 77, 77, 101, 114, 159, 311, 382. n = 8, so average the 4th and 5th values: (101 + 114)/2 = 107.5

(b) Ordered: 2483, 2559, 2821, 4894, 6180. n = 5, so take the 3rd value: 2821

Properties of the median: it finds the centre (middle value) of the data; it is used to tell whether a value falls in the upper or lower half; it can be used for an open-ended distribution; it is affected less than the mean by extremely high or low values. Amani

Median for grouped data Amani

(1) Find the total frequency N. (2) Calculate N/2. (3) Build the cumulative frequency (CF). The class whose CF first exceeds N/2 is the median class.

Drivers: N = 71, so N/2 = 35.5. The CF column reads 19, 39, …, and 39 is the first CF above 35.5. The median class is therefore 29–36 (28.5–36.5). In words: the middle driver is aged between 29 and 36.

Optional: only if a question asks for one number

Interpolating inside the median class gives L + ((N/2 − CFbefore)/f)·w = 28.5 + ((35.5 − 19)/20)·8 = 35.1, where L is the lower boundary, f the class frequency and w the class width. This formula is not in either deck. The course answer is the median class.

4 · The mode Both

The mode is the value that occurs most often: the highest bar in a bar chart or histogram, sometimes described as the most typical case. It works for qualitative and quantitative data. Four cases:

  • Unimodal: one value has the greatest frequency.
  • Bimodal: two values tie for the greatest frequency, and both are modes.
  • Multimodal: more than two values tie.
  • No mode: no value occurs more than once. Never write "the mode is zero".

For grouped data, the class with the highest frequency is the modal class, and its midpoint is sometimes reported as the mode. Properties: the mode is used when the most typical case is wanted; it is the easiest average to compute; it is the only average for nominal or categorical data (religious preference, gender, political affiliation); it is less sensitive to outliers. الشرح بالعربي — المنوالالمنوال هو القيمة الأكثر تكرارًا. قد يكون منوالًا واحدًا (unimodal)، أو اثنين (bimodal)، أو أكثر (multimodal)، أو لا يوجد منوال إذا لم تتكرر أي قيمة. احذر: لا تكتب «المنوال = صفر»، بل اكتب «لا يوجد منوال» (no mode). المنوال هو المقياس الوحيد الذي يصلح للبيانات الوصفية الاسمية مثل فصيلة الدم أو الجنس. في الجداول المبوّبة نحدد الفئة المنوالية، وهي الفئة ذات أعلى تكرار.

5 · The midrange (MR) Hadhrami

Midrange

MR = lowest value + highest value2

A very rough estimate of the average. It uses only the two extremes, so a single outlier distorts it.

Ex 3-12 · Bank failures

3, 30, 148, 157, 71. Find the midrange, then compare it with the mean (in-class exercise).

MR = (3 + 157)/2 = 80    x̄ = 409/5 = 81.8

Here the two are close, but MR ignores the three middle values entirely.

6 · The weighted mean Both

The weighted mean accounts for the relative importance (weight) of each value. Use it when the values are not equally represented, that is, when some values contribute more to the average than others.

Weighted mean

x̄ = w₁X₁ + w₂X₂ + ⋯ + wₙXₙw₁ + w₂ + ⋯ + wₙ = ΣwXΣw

Ex 3-14 · GPA Both

A in English (3 credits), C in Psychology (3), B in Biology (4), D in PE (2). A = 4, B = 3, C = 2, D = 1, F = 0.

x̄ = 3·4 + 3·2 + 4·3 + 2·13 + 3 + 4 + 2 = 3212 = 2.667 ≈ 2.7

8 · Distribution shape Both · CLO3-B

Homework · stem-and-leaf (key 1 | 0 = 10) Amani

1 | 0 2 3 5 5 5 6 7 7 9  ·  2 | 0 2 3  ·  3 |  ·  4 | 5. Find the mean, median and mode, and describe the shape.

As plotted (n = 14, ΣX = 259): x̄ = 18.5; median = mean of the 7th and 8th values = (16 + 17)/2 = 16.5; mode = 15.

Printed answers (19.6, 17, 15) correspond to one extra value, 35 (n = 15, ΣX = 294).

In both versions mode < median < mean: positively (right) skewed. The value 45 forms the long right tail.

Active learning · stem-and-leaf Amani

1 | 2 3 4 · 2 | 3 4 5 6 7 · 3 | 0 1 2 · 4 | 2 4 5 6 7. (1) Minimum and maximum. (2) Mean, median, mode. (3) Shape.

(1) min = 12, max = 47. (2) n = 16, ΣX = 481, so x̄ = 30.06; median = (27 + 30)/2 = 28.5; no mode, since every value appears once.

(3) The mean is only slightly above the median: the distribution is roughly symmetric with a slight right skew. With no mode, compare the mean and median.
