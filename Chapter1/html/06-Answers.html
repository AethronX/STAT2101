Part B · Short exam problems by lesson

Classification with justification, Table D, systematic k, find-the-mistake.

B11-1 CLO1-A

(a) all 3,500 first-year SQU students, N = 3500. (b) the 150 students asked, n = 150. (c) daily study hours: quantitative, continuous, ratio. (d) statistic: it comes from the sample. (e) inferential: the conclusion is about all first-year students.

B21-1 CLO1-A

(a) census: every member of the population. (b) survey: a sample. (c) census: the population is defined as her class, and all of it is measured. Whether something is a census depends on how the population is defined.

B31-2 CLO1-B

(a) qualitative, nominal (b) quantitative, discrete, ratio (c) quantitative, continuous, interval (d) qualitative, ordinal (e) quantitative, continuous, ratio: a duration (f) qualitative, nominal: a label, even though it contains digits.

B41-2 CLO1-B

(a) 47.5–48.5 kg (±0.5) (b) 3.65–3.75 (±0.05) (c) 0.1245–0.1255 s (±0.0005)

Half of one unit of the last recorded digit, each way.

B51-2 CLO1-B · CLO3-C

Invalid. Temperature in °C is interval: 0 °C is not an absence of heat, so ratios are meaningless. Only the difference (+20 °C) is meaningful. On the Kelvin scale, a ratio scale, the temperatures are 293.15 K and 313.15 K, a ratio of only about 1.07.

B61-2 CLO1-B

Student B. Goals are counted, so the variable is discrete however large the counts get; 0 goals means none, so it is ratio. Continuous means measured, with infinitely many values between any two, which is not the same as "can be large".

B71-3 CLO1-C

N has 3 digits, so read 3-digit groups: 065 120 783 688 456 363 133.

Sample: 065, 120, 688, 456, 363, 133. 783 is skipped because it is greater than 750.

B81-3 CLO1-C

(a) k = 250/12 = 20.83, rounded down to 20. Sample: 7, 27, 47, 67, 87, 107, 127, 147, 167, 187, 207, 227.

(b) 21, 42, …, 231, then the 12th would be 252 > 250: only 11 patients. Rounding up can leave the sample short, which is why the rule rounds down.

B91-3 CLO1-C · CLO3-C

An SRS might, by chance, contain too few of one gender to compare reliably. Stratify by gender: list males and females separately, then draw a random sample (for example by Table D or a computer) from each stratum. This guarantees both genders are represented; the two sample sizes may be equal or different.

B101-3 CLO1-C

(a) convenience, biased: early Saturday shoppers are not typical. (b) volunteer, biased: self-selected respondents with strong views. (c) simple random (lottery method), unbiased, provided the slips are well mixed.

B111-3 CLO1-C

P is cluster: cheaper and faster, with only 10 sites to visit. Q is stratified (schools as strata): every school is represented, but all 120 schools must be visited.

B121-3 CLO1-C

Tree 88 does not exist (N = 86). k should be 86/6 = 14.33, rounded down to 14. With start 13: 13, 27, 41, 55, 69, 83.

B131-3 CLO1-C

The groups must have the same number of digits as N, which is 4. 06512 = 6512 > 4998 would be rejected anyway. The correct start is 0651, 2078, 3688, 4563, ….

B141-1 1-3 CLO1-A · CLO1-C · CLO3-C

(a) inferential: a general claim about a relationship. (b) volunteer (self-selected). (c) The sample is biased (club members who chose to answer are not typical students), and 50 people is small. A survey can also only show an association: it doesn't prove that sleeping more causes a higher GPA.

C41-2 CLO1-B · CLO3-C [8 marks]

(i) nominal: only "different from". (ii) ordinal: "different" and "greater than"; a 10-unit difference has no fixed meaning. (iii) interval: different, greater, 10 units more; not twice. (iv) ratio: all four, so OMR 800 is twice OMR 400.

Part D · True / false with justification

The justification is the mark.

D11-2 CLO1-B · CLO1-C

True. Nominal and ordinal data are categories (qualitative); numerical data with meaningful differences are interval or ratio.

D21-2 CLO1-B · CLO1-C

False. The number of children is discrete and ratio: 0 means none, and 4 is twice 2.

D31-3 CLO1-B · CLO1-C

True. Sampling error comes from measuring only part of the population. A census can still have nonsampling errors such as recording mistakes.

D41-3 CLO1-B · CLO1-C

False. A defective instrument or a biased question gives wrong data at any sample size.

D51-3 CLO1-B · CLO1-C

False. Only the randomly chosen clusters do, with all their members. "Some from every group" describes stratified sampling.

D61-3 CLO1-B · CLO1-C

False. Dr. Amani's note: the sizes need not be equal, e.g. 12 from Statistics and 24 from Mathematics.

D71-2 CLO1-B · CLO1-C

False. They are labels; averaging them is meaningless. Qualitative, nominal.

D81-3 CLO1-B · CLO1-C

False. Size does not fix how subjects were chosen. A large biased sample is still biased.
