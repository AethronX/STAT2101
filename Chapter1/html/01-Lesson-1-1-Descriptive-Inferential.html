1 · Why statistics, and what statistics is Both

Statistics is used in almost every field for decision-making: medicine (is a new drug effective?), education (what affects student performance?), business (predicting customer demand). Every one of these needs collected facts and observations, and those are data. Amani

Statistics also reaches you through reports: the average height of Omani males is 171.70 cm and of females 158.44 cm; diabetes accounts for more than 15% of deaths among people aged 60 and over in Bahrain and Qatar; 51.7% of SQU students admitted in 2021–22 were in humanities colleges. Ahmed

Definition (exam wording)

Statistics is the science of conducting studies to collect, organize, summarize, analyze, and draw conclusions from data.

In your own research you must be able to design experiments; collect, organize, analyze and summarize data; make reliable predictions or forecasts; and communicate the results in your own words. Ahmed

3 · Population, sample, census, survey Both · CLO1-A

A population consists of all subjects or objects being studied, and must be clearly defined. Its size is N. Examples: all students at SQU; all cars a company produced in 2024; all registered STAT 2101 students; all SQU female students living with their families; all Lexus cars owned by SQU employees.

A sample is a group selected from the population, a subset of it. Its size is n. It must represent the population, so its subjects must be properly selected. Examples: 200 SQU students selected for a study; 150 cars tested for safety; 76 students randomly selected from the STAT 2101 list.

Collecting data from every member of the population is a census, such as Oman's General Census of Population, Housing and Establishments 2020 by the NCSI. Collecting from a sample is a survey.

Why samples? Expense, time, the size of the population, medical concerns and similar reasons usually make a census impossible.

A biased sample does not represent the population it came from. Example: choosing the students who arrive early to class. Ahmed

4 · The two branches Both · CLO1-A

Descriptive statistics

Consists of the collection, organization, summarization, and presentation of data, from a sample or a population. Researchers describe a situation: collect data, organize and summarize it, and present it as charts, graphs or tables (Chapters 2 and 3).

Inferential statistics

Consists of generalizing from samples to populations, performing estimations and hypothesis tests, determining relationships among variables, and making predictions. It is built on probability (Ch. 4–6), estimation and testing (Ch. 7–9), and relationships and prediction (Ch. 10). Its purpose is to make decisions and draw conclusions about the population.

Example 1-1 Ahmed

Descriptive or inferential? (a) The average price of a 30-second Academy Awards ad was $1.90 million. (b) The Department of Economic and Social Affairs predicts Mexico City's population in 2030 will be 238,647,000. (c) A medical report: statins lower heart attacks, but some people are at slightly higher diabetes risk. (d) A Harris Poll of 2234 people: 55% said excessive complaining by adults was the most annoying social media habit.

(a) Descriptive: an average of observed data. (b) Inferential: a prediction. (c) Inferential: a general conclusion about everyone from studies. (d) Descriptive: a percentage of the 2234 people actually asked.

Activity · statistics type Amani

(1) The Oman Meteorology Department records daily temperature variation in different cities and publishes a report on monthly weather patterns. (2) The Ministry of Tourism surveys visitor satisfaction at major attractions and uses the results to predict overall tourist satisfaction for the country.

(1) Descriptive: it reports what was recorded. (2) Inferential: a sample (visitors surveyed) is used to predict for the whole country.

Dr. Amani's diagram examples: the Oman Census 2020 average age and age frequency table are descriptive; the Ministry of Health surveying a sample of hospitals in November to predict next November's national flu cases is inferential.
