4-1

Sample Spaces and Probability فضاءات العينة والاحتمال

The whole of probability is one idea executed carefully: name every outcome that could happen, name the ones you care about, compare the two counts. Everything in §4-1 is machinery for doing that without losing an outcome.

2 · Writing sample spaces — the three standard devices

الشرح بالعربي — كيف أكتب فضاء العينة؟

قبل حساب أي احتمال يجب أن تكتب فضاء العينة كاملًا دون أن تنسى أي نتيجة، لأن المقام في كل قانون هو n(S). توجد ثلاث طرق:

  1. الجدول: مناسب لرمي حجري نرد — 6 × 6 = 36 نتيجة على شكل أزواج مرتبة.
  2. السرد المنظّم: تكتب النتائج بترتيب ثابت (كل الأولاد أولًا، ثم بنت واحدة في كل موقع، ثم بنتان...) حتى لا تسقط نتيجة.
  3. شجرة الاحتمالات: كل مرحلة تتفرع إلى احتمالاتها، وعدد الأطراف النهائية = عدد النتائج. الشجرة هي الدليل على أنك لم تنسَ شيئًا.

Device 1 — The grid (two dice)

Example 4-1 · Rolling Dice

Find the sample space for rolling two dice.

Each die has 6 faces, and the dice are distinguishable (one red, one white). Lay them out as a 6 × 6 grid of ordered pairs:

S = {(1,1), (1,2), …, (1,6), (2,1), …, (6,6)}

So n(S) = 36

Why it matters: every dice question in the chapter divides by 36. Memorise the sum distribution now — sum 7 occurs 6 times, sums 6 and 8 occur 5 times each, 5 and 9 four times, 4 and 10 three times, 3 and 11 twice, 2 and 12 once. Total 36. ✔

Device 2 — Systematic listing (gender of children)

Example 4-3 · Gender of Children

Find the sample space for the gender of the children if a family has three children. Use B for boy and G for girl.

S = {BBB, BBG, BGB, GBB, BGG, GBG, GGB, GGG}

n(S) = 8   (= 2 × 2 × 2, by the Fundamental Counting Rule of §4-4)

Note the ordering discipline: all-boys first, then one girl in each of three positions, then two girls in each of three positions, then all girls. Listing in a fixed order is how you avoid dropping an outcome under exam pressure.

3 · Venn diagrams and set operations

A Venn diagram is a graphical way of representing the relationships between sets. The rectangle is S; circles inside it are events. Three standard configurations: overlapping (A ∩ B ≠ ∅), disjoint (A ∩ B = ∅), and nested (B ⊂ A).

The three set operations

Union   A ∪ B  —  read "A or B" — all outcomes in either event or both.

Intersection   A ∩ B  —  read "A and B" — all outcomes in both events.

Complement   Ā  or  Ac  —  read "not A" — all outcomes in S that are not in A.

In probability, "or" is always inclusive: A or B or both. This is not the everyday English "or". It is the single most common source of lost marks in §4-2.

الشرح بالعربي — مخطط فن والعمليات على الأحداث

مخطط فن (Venn diagram): مستطيل يمثل فضاء العينة S، وبداخله دوائر تمثل الأحداث.

  • الاتحاد A ∪ B ويُقرأ A or B: كل العناصر الموجودة في A أو في B أو في الاثنين معًا.
  • التقاطع A ∩ B ويُقرأ A and B: العناصر المشتركة فقط بين الحدثين.
  • المتمّمة Ā أو Aᶜ وتُقرأ not A: كل عناصر S التي ليست في A.

تنبيه: كلمة or في الاحتمال تعني «أحدهما أو كلاهما» دائمًا، وليست «أحدهما فقط» كما في الكلام اليومي. والمتمّمة تُحسب من داخل S فقط — في المثال أعلاه الرقم 8 يدخل في Ā لأنه ليس من عوامل 12.

Worked example · 12-sided die

Roll a 12-sided die once. S = {1,…,12}. Let A = factors of 12 and B = factors of 8. Represent S, A, B on a Venn diagram, then find (a) A ∪ B, (b) A ∩ B, (c) Ā, (d) (A ∪ B)c.

First, build the sets. Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 → A = {1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12}. Factors of 8: 1, 2, 4, 8 → B = {1, 2, 4, 8}.

Venn layout. Inside A only: 3, 6, 12. Inside both: 1, 2, 4. Inside B only: 8. Outside both, still in S: 5, 7, 9, 10, 11.

  1. (a) A ∪ B = {1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12}  →  n = 7
  2. (b) A ∩ B = {1, 2, 4}  →  n = 3
  3. (c) Ā = {5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11}  →  n = 6  (everything in S not in A — note 8 is included)
  4. (d) (A ∪ B)c = {5, 7, 9, 10, 11}  →  n = 5

Check: n(A ∪ B) + n((A ∪ B)c) = 7 + 5 = 12 = n(S). ✔ Always run this check — it catches a dropped element in two seconds.

Trap — the complement must stay inside S

Ā is not "everything else in the universe". It is everything in this sample space that is not in A. In the example above, students routinely forget 8 belongs in Ā because it "feels like it belongs to B". B is irrelevant to Ā.

4 · The three interpretations of probability

Probability is used to quantify the likelihood, or chance, that an outcome of a random experiment will occur. The probability that event A occurs is written P(A). There are three basic interpretations:

1 · Classical

Uses sample spaces. Assumes all outcomes are equally likely. Computed before any data is collected — pure counting.

2 · Empirical (relative frequency)

Based on observation. Computed from a frequency distribution of data already collected.

3 · Subjective

A value based on an educated guess or estimate, employing opinions and inexact information.

الشرح بالعربي — أنواع الاحتمال الثلاثة
  • الكلاسيكي (Classical): يُحسب من فضاء العينة قبل إجراء أي تجربة، بشرط أن تكون كل النتائج متساوية الفرص. مثال: احتمال ظهور 3 عند رمي نرد = 1/6.
  • التجريبي (Empirical): يُحسب من بيانات جُمعت فعلًا — جدول تكراري أو استبيان — ويساوي التكرار ÷ المجموع.
  • الشخصي (Subjective): تقدير مبني على رأي أو خبرة شخص، بلا عدّ وبلا بيانات.

طريقة التمييز في الامتحان: فضاء عينة يُعدّ ← كلاسيكي. جدول أو «في استبيان شمل n شخصًا» ← تجريبي. رأي خبير ← شخصي.

Classical probability

Equally likely events are events that have the same probability of occurring. The classical probability of any event E of a sample space S is:

Classical probability

P(E) = n(E)n(S)

n(E) = the number of outcomes in E.  n(S) = the number of outcomes in S.

Example 4-6 · Gender of Children

If a family has three children, find the probability that exactly two of the three children are girls.

  1. Sample space: S = {BBB, BBG, BGB, GBB, BGG, GBG, GGB, GGG}, so n(S) = 8.
  2. Event: E = "exactly two of the three children are girls" = {BGG, GBG, GGB}, so n(E) = 3.
  3. Divide: P(E) = n(E)/n(S) = 3/8 = 0.375

Important (the lecturer flags this explicitly): sample spaces and events must be written out using set notation as shown above. Marks are allocated to the set, not only to the final number. Writing just "3/8" loses method marks.

The four probability rules

Probability rules — let event E be in sample space S

(1)   0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1

(2)   If P(E) = 0, then E cannot happen (impossible).

(3)   If P(E) = 1, then E is certain.

(4)   The sum of the probabilities of all outcomes in S equals 1.

The probability line: 0 = Impossible · ~0.25 = Unlikely · 0.5 = 50–50 · ~0.75 = Likely · 1 = Certain.

الشرح بالعربي — القانون الكلاسيكي وقواعد الاحتمال

الاحتمال الكلاسيكي = عدد النتائج التي تحقق الحدث ÷ عدد كل النتائج الممكنة: P(E) = n(E) / n(S).

قواعد الاحتمال الأربع:

  1. الاحتمال دائمًا بين الصفر والواحد: 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1.
  2. إذا كان P(E) = 0 فالحدث مستحيل (مثل ظهور 9 على نرد عادي).
  3. إذا كان P(E) = 1 فالحدث مؤكد (مثل ظهور عدد أقل من 7).
  4. مجموع احتمالات كل النتائج في فضاء العينة = 1.

استخدم القاعدة الأولى لفحص كل إجابة: أي احتمال سالب أو أكبر من 1 يعني أن في الحل خطأ. وتذكّر أن الدكتور يطلب كتابة فضاء العينة والحدث بصيغة المجموعات {…} — عليها درجات.

Examples 4-8 & 4-9 · Rolling a Die

(a) When a single die is rolled, find the probability of getting a 9.  (b) When a single die is rolled, what is the probability of getting a number less than 7?

(a) S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}; E: getting a 9. It is impossible to get a 9, so E = { } and n(E) = 0.

P(E) = 0/6 = 0

(b) S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}; E: getting a number less than 7. Every face qualifies, so E = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6} and n(E) = 6.

P(E) = 6/6 = 1 — the event is certain.

These two examples exist to demonstrate rules (2) and (3). An exam question that produces P = 0 or P = 1 is testing whether you recognise impossibility and certainty — say the words in your answer.

5 · Complementary events

The complement of an event E is the set of outcomes in the sample space that are not included in the outcomes of event E. It is denoted Ē (read "E bar").

Rule for complementary events

P(Ē) = 1 − P(E)   or   P(E) = 1 − P(Ē)   or   P(E) + P(Ē) = 1

This is the highest-leverage formula in the chapter. Whenever a question contains "not", "at least one", "neither", or "none", reach for it before you do anything else.

الشرح بالعربي — المتمّمة

متمّمة الحدث E هي كل النتائج التي لا تحقق E. وبما أن الحدث إما أن يقع أو لا يقع، فمجموع الاحتمالين = 1، ومنه:

P(Ē) = 1 − P(E)

متى تستخدمها؟ عندما ترى في السؤال: not (ليس)، neither … nor (لا هذا ولا ذاك)، none (ولا واحد)، at least one (واحد على الأقل). هذه القاعدة كثيرًا ما تحوّل سؤالًا طويلًا إلى عملية طرح واحدة. مثال: 23% يفضّلون الفانيلا، إذن احتمال ليس الفانيلا = 1 − 0.23 = 0.77.

Example 4-10 · Finding Complements

Find the complement of each event: (a) selecting a month that has 30 days; (b) selecting a day of the week that begins with the letter S; (c) rolling two dice and getting a number whose sum is 7.

  1. (a) Months have 28, 30 or 31 days. Complement = selecting a month that has 28 or 31 days.
  2. (b) Days starting with S are Saturday and Sunday. Complement = selecting a day that does not begin with the letter S — that is, Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday or Friday.
  3. (c) The possible sums of two dice are 2 through 12. Complement = getting a sum of 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 11, or 12.
Note on the slide

The lecture slide for part (b) prints "does not begin with the letter T", which is a typo carried from an earlier version of the example. The event is "begins with S", so its complement is "does not begin with S". Write S in your answer and you are correct against the textbook.

Example 4-11 · Favorite Ice Cream Flavors

In a study, 23% of the people surveyed said that vanilla was their favorite flavor of ice cream. If a person is selected at random, find the probability that the person's favorite flavor is not vanilla.

Let E be "favorite flavor is vanilla", with P(E) = 0.23.

P(not vanilla) = 1 − P(E) = 1 − 0.23 = 0.77  (or 77%)

6 · Empirical probability

Given a frequency distribution, the probability of an event being in a given class is the relative frequency of that class:

Empirical (relative frequency) probability

P(E) = frequency for the classtotal frequencies in the distribution = fn

f = frequency of the class (event); n = the sample size. Based on observation, not on assuming equally likely outcomes.

الشرح بالعربي — الاحتمال التجريبي

يُحسب من البيانات المجموعة: P(E) = f / n، حيث f تكرار الفئة و n حجم العينة الكلي. مثال: سألت 50 شخصًا وأجاب 3 منهم «قطار أو حافلة»، فالاحتمال 3/50 = 0.06.

نصيحة: اجمع عمود التكرارات وتأكد أنه يساوي n، واجمع عمود الاحتمالات وتأكد أنه يساوي 1. وإذا طُلب احتمال فئتين معًا (A أو B) فاجمع تكراريهما ثم اقسم.

Example 4-12 · Travel Survey

A researcher asked 50 people who plan to travel over the Thanksgiving holiday how they will get to their destination: Drive 41, Fly 6, Train or bus 3. Find the probability that a person will travel by train or bus.

Example 4-14 · Sleep Hours

A survey found the following distribution for hours of sleep per night: Less than 6 h → 12; 6 h → 26; 7 h → 30; 8 h → 28; More than 8 h → 4. Total 100. Find, for a person selected at random: (a) sleeps 8 hours; (b) sleeps fewer than 7 hours; (c) sleeps at most 8 hours; (d) sleeps at least 7 hours.

  1. (a) Exactly 8 hours — one class only: P = 28/100 = 0.28
  2. (b) Fewer than 7 hours — strictly below 7, so "less than 6" and "6": P = (12 + 26)/100 = 38/100 = 0.38

8 · Subjective probability

Subjective probability uses a probability value based on an educated guess or estimate, employing opinions and inexact information. No sample space is counted and no data is tabulated — a qualified person simply assigns a number.

  • A sportswriter may say there is a 70% probability that the Oman football team will win the Gulf Cup next year.
  • A physician might say that, on the basis of her diagnosis, there is a 30% chance the patient will need an operation.
  • A seismologist might say there is an 80% probability that an earthquake will occur in a certain area.
الشرح بالعربي — الاحتمال الشخصي

رقم يعطيه شخص بناءً على خبرته أو رأيه، مثل قول محلل رياضي إن احتمال فوز منتخب عُمان بكأس الخليج 70%، أو قول طبيب إن احتمال حاجة المريض لعملية 30%. لا يوجد فضاء عينة يُعدّ ولا جدول بيانات — مجرد تقدير مدروس. غالبًا يأتي في الامتحان كسؤال: «ما نوع هذا الاحتمال؟»

How this is examined

You will be shown a statement and asked which type of probability is this? The tell: counting a sample space → classical. A frequency table or "in a survey of n people" → empirical. An expert's opinion with no data behind it → subjective. One mark, two seconds, never miss it.

4-2

The Addition Rules for Probability قواعد الجمع للاحتمال

§4-2 answers exactly one question: what is P(A or B)? There are two formulas, and choosing between them depends on a single yes/no test.

1 · Mutually exclusive events — the test

Two events are said to be mutually exclusive events (or disjoint) if they cannot occur at the same time — that is, they have no elements in common.

The test

A and B are mutually exclusive  ⟺  A ∩ B = ∅

∅ is the null (empty) set. On a Venn diagram, mutually exclusive events are drawn as two circles that do not overlap.

الشرح بالعربي — الأحداث المتنافية

الحدثان المتنافيان (mutually exclusive أو disjoint) هما حدثان لا يمكن أن يقعا في نفس الوقت، أي لا يوجد بينهما أي عنصر مشترك: A ∩ B = ∅. على مخطط فن تكون الدائرتان منفصلتين تمامًا.

مثال: عند رمي نرد، «عدد زوجي» {2, 4, 6} و«عدد فردي» {1, 3, 5} متنافيان. أما «عدد زوجي» و«عامل من عوامل 4» {1, 2, 4} فغير متنافيين لأن 2 و 4 مشتركان.

طريقة الإجابة الكاملة: اكتب التقاطع صراحةً ثم احكم — لا تكتفِ بـ «نعم» أو «لا».

Worked example · Rolling one die

S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}. A = getting an even number = {2, 4, 6}; B = getting an odd number = {1, 3, 5}; C = getting a factor of 4 = {1, 2, 4}. (a) Are A and B mutually exclusive? (b) Are A and C mutually exclusive?

  1. (a) A ∩ B = {2,4,6} ∩ {1,3,5} = ∅.  Yes — mutually exclusive. A number cannot be even and odd at once.
  2. (b) A ∩ C = {2,4,6} ∩ {1,2,4} = {2, 4} ≠ ∅.  No — not mutually exclusive. Rolling a 2 or a 4 satisfies both events simultaneously.

Method mark: always write the intersection out before you answer. "Yes/No" alone is worth less than "A ∩ C = {2, 4} ≠ ∅, therefore not mutually exclusive."

Example 4-15 · Determining Mutually Exclusive Events

Determine whether the two events are mutually exclusive, and explain your answer.
(a) Randomly selecting a female student / randomly selecting a student who is a second year.
(b) Randomly selecting a person with type A blood / randomly selecting a person with type O blood.
(c) Rolling a die and getting an odd number / rolling a die and getting a number less than 3.
(d) Randomly selecting a person who is under 21 years of age / randomly selecting a person who is over 30 years of age.

  1. (a) Not mutually exclusive. A student can be female and a second year at the same time.
  2. (b) Mutually exclusive. A person has exactly one blood type; being type A excludes being type O.
  3. (c) Not mutually exclusive. Odd = {1, 3, 5}; less than 3 = {1, 2}. They share the outcome 1.
  4. (d) Mutually exclusive. No one is simultaneously under 21 and over 30.

2 · The two addition rules

Addition Rule #1 — A and B mutually exclusive

P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B)

Addition Rule #2 — A and B NOT mutually exclusive

P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B)

The overlap gets counted once in P(A) and once again in P(B), so it must be subtracted once to be counted correctly. On the Venn diagram this is the shaded lens in the middle.

الشرح بالعربي — قاعدتا الجمع

قاعدة الجمع تجيب عن سؤال واحد: ما احتمال A أو B؟

  • إذا كان الحدثان متنافيين: نجمع مباشرة P(A) + P(B).
  • إذا كانا غير متنافيين: نجمع ثم نطرح التقاطع مرة واحدة: P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B)، لأن المنطقة المشتركة حُسبت مرتين — مرة داخل A ومرة داخل B.

مثال: 8 ممرضين و 5 أطباء؛ منهم ممرض واحد ذكر وطبيبان ذكور. احتمال (ممرض أو ذكر) = 8/13 + 3/13 − 1/13 = 10/13، لأن الممرض الذكر دخل في العدّ مرتين.

Use Rule #2 always — it is safe

Rule #1 is a special case of Rule #2, because when A and B are mutually exclusive, P(A ∩ B) = 0 and the subtraction disappears. If you are ever unsure under exam pressure, write Rule #2 and show that the intersection is zero. You cannot be wrong, and you gain a method mark for justifying it.

Example 4-17 · Endangered Species

In the United States there are 59 species of mammals that are endangered, 75 species of birds, and 68 species of fish. If one animal is selected at random, find the probability that it is either a mammal or a fish.

  1. Total: n(S) = 59 + 75 + 68 = 202.
  2. Mutually exclusive? An animal cannot be both a mammal and a fish → yes, so use Rule #1.
  3. P(mammal ∪ fish) = 59202 + 68202 = 127202 ≈ 0.629

Example 4-22 · Driving While Depressed

The phrase "having a driving accident while depressed" means both things happen to the same person: P(D ∩ A) = 0.06. It does not mean P(A | D). 3 · Extended addition rules (three or more events)

Three mutually exclusive events A, B, C

P(A ∪ B ∪ C) = P(A) + P(B) + P(C)

Three events that are NOT mutually exclusive

P(A ∪ B ∪ C) = P(A) + P(B) + P(C) − P(A ∩ B) − P(A ∩ C) − P(B ∩ C) + P(A ∩ B ∩ C)

Pattern: add the singles, subtract the pairs, add back the triple. The lecture notes point you to Exercises 19–21 for the mutually exclusive version and Exercise 24 for the general version.

متى أستخدم أي قاعدة؟ اسأل نفسك سؤالًا واحدًا: هل يمكن للحدثين أن يقعا معًا؟
• لا ← متنافيان (mutually exclusive) ← P(A) + P(B)
• نعم ← غير متنافيين ← P(A) + P(B) − P(A∩B)
وإذا ترددت، استخدم القاعدة الثانية دائمًا — فهي تعطي النتيجة الصحيحة في الحالتين لأن التقاطع يساوي صفرًا عند التنافي.

The Multiplication Rules and Conditional Probability قواعد الضرب والاحتمال الشرطي

§4-2 handled "or". §4-3 handles "and" — and then inverts it to handle "given that". This is the densest section in the chapter and the one that separates a B from an A.

1 · Independent vs dependent events

Independent events

Two events A and B are independent if the fact that A occurs does not affect the probability of B occurring.

Dependent events

Two events A and B are dependent if the fact that A occurs does affect the probability of B occurring.

Important note — independent ≠ mutually exclusive

These two ideas are constantly confused and the lecturer calls it out explicitly. When tossing a single coin, the result can be either heads or tails but cannot be both — those are mutually exclusive events. When tossing two coins, the result of one flip does not affect the result of the other — those are independent events.

The deeper point: mutually exclusive events (with non-zero probabilities) are in fact dependent. If A happens, you know for certain B did not — knowing about A changed everything about B. Mutually exclusive is about overlap; independent is about influence.

الشرح بالعربي — أهم فكرة في القسم: الاستقلال والتبعية
  • المستقلان (independent): وقوع أحدهما لا يغيّر احتمال الآخر. مثال: رمي عملتين — نتيجة الأولى لا تؤثر في الثانية.
  • التابعان (dependent): وقوع أحدهما يغيّر احتمال الآخر. مثال: سحب كرتين بدون إرجاع — السحبة الأولى تغيّر ما تبقى في الصندوق.

لا تخلط بين المفهومين: «متنافيان» سؤاله «هل يوجد تداخل؟»، و«مستقلان» سؤاله «هل يؤثر أحدهما في الآخر؟». بل إن الحدثين المتنافيين (باحتمالات غير صفرية) يكونان تابعين، لأن وقوع A يجعل وقوع B مستحيلًا — أي أنه أثّر فيه بالكامل.

Spotting dependence in a word problem

  • "…and then it is replaced" → independent. The pool resets, so probabilities repeat.
  • "…without replacement" → dependent. Both the numerator and the denominator shrink on the second draw.
  • Separate devices (a coin and a die, three different people chosen from a large population) → independent.
  • "Of these clients…", "of those who…" → dependent; you are being given a conditional probability.
كيف أعرف من صياغة السؤال؟
  • with replacement / then it is replaced (مع الإرجاع) ← مستقلان، الاحتمال يتكرر كما هو.
  • without replacement (بدون إرجاع) ← تابعان، البسط والمقام يتغيران في السحبة الثانية.
  • أدوات منفصلة (عملة ونرد، أشخاص من مجتمع كبير) ← مستقلان.
  • of these / of those who (من بين هؤلاء) ← هذا احتمال شرطي، والحدثان تابعان.

2 · The multiplication rules

Multiplication Rule #1 — independent events

P(A ∩ B) = P(A) · P(B)

Multiplication Rule #2 — dependent events

P(A ∩ B) = P(A | B) · P(B)

P(A ∩ B) = P(B | A) · P(A)

Both forms are correct — use whichever conditional probability the question hands you. In practice the question gives one unconditional probability and one conditional one; multiply them.

الشرح بالعربي — قاعدتا الضرب

قاعدة الضرب تجيب عن سؤال: ما احتمال A و B معًا؟

  • إذا كانا مستقلين: P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B). مثال: صورة في العملة و 4 في النرد = 1/2 × 1/6 = 1/12.
  • إذا كانا تابعين: P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B | A)، أي احتمال الأول × احتمال الثاني بشرط وقوع الأول. مثال: 53% لديهم تأمين منزل، ومن بين هؤلاء 27% لديهم تأمين سيارة ← 0.53 × 0.27 = 0.1431.

القاعدة الذهبية: and تعني ضرب، و or تعني جمع.

Example 4-23 · Tossing a Coin

A coin is flipped and a die is rolled. Find the probability of getting a head on the coin and a 4 on the die.

  1. The coin and the die do not influence each other → independent → Rule #1.
  2. P(head) = 1/2   and   P(4) = 1/6.
  3. P(head ∩ 4) = 12 · 16 = 112 ≈ 0.0833

Verify by sample space: n(S) = 2 × 6 = 12 outcomes, and only (H, 4) qualifies → 1/12. ✔

Example 4-25 · Selecting a Colored Ball

An urn contains 3 red balls, 2 blue balls, and 5 white balls. A ball is selected and its color noted. Then it is replaced. A second ball is selected and its color noted. Find the probability of: (a) selecting 2 blue balls; (b) selecting 1 blue ball and then 1 white ball; (c) selecting 1 red ball and then 1 blue ball.

Total balls = 3 + 2 + 5 = 10. The ball is replaced, so every draw sees the same 10 balls → independent → Rule #1.

  1. (a) P(B ∩ B) = 210 · 210 = 4100 = 125 = 0.04
  2. (b) P(B ∩ W) = 210 · 510 = 10100 = 0.10
  3. (c) P(R ∩ B) = 310 · 210 = 6100 = 0.06
The variant you must be ready for

If the same question said "without replacement", every second denominator becomes 9 and the matching numerator drops by one where the colors agree:
(a) (2/10)(1/9) = 2/90 ≈ 0.0222  ·  (b) (2/10)(5/9) = 10/90 ≈ 0.1111  ·  (c) (3/10)(2/9) = 6/90 ≈ 0.0667.
Examiners pair these two versions constantly. Circle the words "replaced" or "without replacement" the moment you see them.

Example 4-29 · Homeowner's and Automobile Insurance

World Wide Insurance Company found that 53% of the residents of a city had homeowner's insurance (H) with the company. Of these clients, 27% also had automobile insurance (A). If a resident is selected at random, find the probability that the resident has both homeowner's and automobile insurance with World Wide.

  1. Extract carefully. P(H) = 0.53. The phrase "of these clients" makes the 27% conditional: P(A | H) = 0.27.
  2. Dependent → Rule #2.
  3. P(H ∩ A) = P(H) · P(A | H) = 0.53 × 0.27 = 0.1431
Trap — "of these" is the conditional signal

Example 4-28 · Unemployed Workers

For a specific year, 5.2% of U.S. workers were unemployed. During that time, 33% of those who were unemployed received unemployment benefits. If a person is selected at random, find the probability that she or he received unemployment benefits if the person is unemployed.

Extract: P(U) = 0.052, and "33% of those who were unemployed" gives P(B | U) = 0.33.

As worded on the slide ("…if the person is unemployed"), the question asks for the conditional probability, which is handed to you directly:

P(B | U) = 0.33

The textbook version of this example asks for the probability that a randomly selected person "was unemployed and received benefits", which is the multiplication rule:

P(U ∩ B) = P(U) · P(B | U) = 0.052 × 0.33 = 0.01716 ≈ 0.0172

Be ready for both

This pair is the clearest illustration in the chapter of how one word changes the whole calculation. "And" → multiply the two values (0.0172). "Given / if / of those who" → the conditional is already there (0.33). In an exam, write which one you are computing in words before you compute it.

4 · Conditional probability

The conditional probability of an event B in relationship to an event A is the probability that event B occurs after event A has already occurred.

Conditional probability

P(B | A) = P(A ∩ B)P(A)     P(A) ≠ 0

Read it as: the condition goes in the denominator. Conditioning on A means you throw away everything outside A and treat A as the new sample space.

Conditional probability and independence

If A and B are independent:   P(B | A) = P(A ∩ B)P(A) = P(B)

Knowing A happened tells you nothing new about B — which is exactly what "independent" means. This doubles as the test for independence.

الشرح بالعربي — الاحتمال الشرطي

P(B | A) يُقرأ «احتمال B بشرط أن A قد وقع». معناه أننا نعرف مسبقًا أن A حدث، فنحصر أنفسنا داخل A فقط ونعتبره فضاء العينة الجديد.

P(B | A) = P(A ∩ B) ÷ P(A) — الشرط دائمًا في المقام

مثال: احتمال أن يوقف سام سيارته في مكان ممنوع و يحصل على مخالفة 0.06، واحتمال أن يوقفها في مكان ممنوع 0.20. إذا أوقفها فعلًا في مكان ممنوع، فاحتمال المخالفة = 0.06 ÷ 0.20 = 0.30.

اختبار الاستقلال: إذا كان P(B | A) = P(B) — أو P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B) — فالحدثان مستقلان، أي أن معرفة A لم تغيّر شيئًا.

كلمات الشرط في السؤال: given that، if، of those who، أو حقيقة مذكورة على أنها وقعت («وصل سام واضطر للوقوف في مكان ممنوع»).

Example 4-32 · Selecting Colored Chips

A box contains black chips and white chips. A person selects two chips without replacement. If the probability of selecting a black chip and a white chip is 15/56, and the probability of selecting a black chip on the first draw is 3/8, find the probability of selecting the white chip on the second draw, given that the first chip selected was a black chip.

  1. Extract: P(B ∩ W) = 15/56, P(B) = 3/8. Required: P(W | B).
  2. P(W | B) = P(B ∩ W)P(B) = 15/563/8 = 1556 × 83 = 120168 = 57 ≈ 0.714

Dividing by a fraction: flip the bottom fraction and multiply. 15/56 ÷ 3/8 = 15/56 × 8/3. Cancel: 15/3 = 5 and 8/56 = 1/7 → 5/7.

Example 4-33 · Parking Tickets

The probability that Sam parks in a no-parking zone and gets a parking ticket is 0.06, and the probability that Sam cannot find a legal parking space and has to park in the no-parking zone is 0.20. On Tuesday, Sam arrives at school and has to park in a no-parking zone. Find the probability that he will get a parking ticket.

  1. Extract: Let N = parks in a no-parking zone, T = gets a ticket. P(N ∩ T) = 0.06, P(N) = 0.20.
  2. "He has to park in a no-parking zone" = N has already happened → the question wants P(T | N).
  3. P(T | N) = P(N ∩ T)P(N) = 0.060.20 = 0.30

Why this is the exam's favourite shape: the condition is hidden inside a story ("he has to park there") rather than announced with the word "given". Learn to spot a condition stated as an accomplished fact.

6 · Probabilities for "at least" — the complement shortcut

This is the technique the lecture notes flag under "Other Examples: Probabilities for At Least" (textbook Examples 4-36 Rolling a Die and 4-37 Ties, set for self-study). It is the single most reusable trick in the chapter.

The "at least one" rule

P(at least one) = 1 − P(none)

"At least one" has many ways to happen (exactly one, exactly two, exactly three, …) but its complement — "none" — has exactly one. Compute the easy side and subtract.

الشرح بالعربي — «واحد على الأقل»

«واحد على الأقل» له طرق كثيرة للحدوث (واحد، اثنان، ثلاثة...)، لكن عكسه «ولا واحد» له طريقة واحدة فقط. لذلك:

P(at least one) = 1 − P(none)

مثال: رمي نرد 3 مرات، احتمال ظهور 5 مرة واحدة على الأقل = 1 − (5/6)³ = 1 − 125/216 ≈ 0.4213.

كلما رأيت at least في السؤال، فكّر في المتمّمة قبل أي شيء آخر.

Illustration · the standard shape

A single die is rolled 3 times. Find the probability of getting at least one 5.

  1. The long way (don't): P(exactly one 5) + P(exactly two 5s) + P(three 5s). Three separate calculations.
  2. The complement way: "at least one 5" has complement "no 5s at all". P(not a 5 on one roll) = 5/6.
  3. P(no 5s in 3 rolls) = (5/6)3 = 125216
  4. P(at least one 5) = 1 − 125216 = 91216 ≈ 0.4213

The same pattern solves "at least one defective", "at least one female", "at least one match" — see Example 4-51(d) in §4-5, where it appears in its examined form.

The three complement triggers

Reach for 1 − P(…) the moment you read: "at least one" (complement = none) · "not" (complement = the event itself) · "neither / none" (complement = at least one). Scanning for these three phrases before you start writing will save you minutes on every paper.

1 · The Fundamental Counting Rule

Fundamental counting rule

In a sequence of n events in which the first has k1 possibilities, the second has k2, the third has k3, and so on, the total number of possibilities of the sequence is

k1 × k2 × ⋯ × kn

الشرح بالعربي — قاعدة العدّ الأساسية

إذا كان لديك سلسلة من الاختيارات — الأول له k₁ طريقة، والثاني k₂، والثالث k₃... — فعدد كل الاحتمالات = حاصل ضربها.

مثال: 7 ألوان × 2 نوع × 3 ملمس × 2 استخدام = 84 نوع دهان.

  • مع التكرار (repetition allowed): الأرقام تبقى كما هي من اليسار لليمين ← 10 × 10 × 10 × 10 = 10,000.
  • بدون تكرار (not allowed): الأرقام تنقص واحدًا في كل خانة ← 10 × 9 × 8 × 7 = 5,040.

A license plate has a four-digit identification number. How many different plates can be made if: (a) digits are from 0 to 9 and repetitions are permitted? (b) digits are from 0 to 9 and repetitions are not permitted?

  2. (b) Without repetition. Each digit used is removed from the pool:

The lecturer's own rule of thumb

"If repetitions are permitted, then the numbers stay the same going from left to right. If repetitions are not permitted, then the numbers decrease by 1 for each place left to right." Write this on your formula sheet exactly as stated — it answers half of all counting-rule questions on sight.

Example 4-43 · Business Location

(a) A business owner has a choice of 5 locations in which to establish her business. She decides to rank each location according to certain criteria, such as price of the store and parking facilities. How many different ways can she rank the 5 locations?
(b) Suppose she wishes to rank only the top 3 locations selected from 5 locations. How many different ways can she rank them?

  1. (a) All 5 ranked, order matters: 5! = 5 · 4 · 3 · 2 · 1 = 120 ways
    Equivalently: 5P5 = 5!/0! = 120/1 = 120
  2. (b) 3 chosen from 5, order matters: 5P3 = 5!(5 − 3)! = 1202 = 60 ways

Example 4-45 · School Musical Plays

A school musical director can select 2 musical plays to present next year. One will be presented in the fall, and one will be presented in the spring. If she has 9 to pick from, how many different possibilities are there?

The two slots are distinguishable (fall vs spring), so order matters → permutation.

9P2 = 9!(9 − 2)! = 362,8805,040 = 72 possibilities

Why not a combination? Play X in fall + play Y in spring is a different programme from Y in fall + X in spring. Labelled slots always mean permutation.

Example 4-42 · Lock Codes

A person needs to make up a four-digit combination to open the lock on the garage door. The person decides to use four different digits from the first four digits, 0 through 3. How many different combinations can be made?

Four distinct digits arranged in all four positions — a full permutation:

4! = 4 · 3 · 2 · 1 = 24 combinations

Note the vocabulary trap: everyday English calls this a "combination lock", but mathematically it is a permutation — 0123 and 3210 open different locks. The word "combination" in a question is never evidence; only the question "does order matter?" decides.

Permutation Rule 2 — when some objects are identical

nPr₁ r₂ r₃ … rp = n!r1! · r2! · r3! ⋯ rp!

where r1 + r2 + ⋯ + rp = n. Divide out the arrangements you cannot tell apart.

الشرح بالعربي — تباديل مع عناصر متكررة

إذا كانت بعض الأشياء متطابقة (مثل الحروف المكررة في كلمة)، نقسم n! على مضروب عدد تكرار كل عنصر، لأن تبديل عنصرين متطابقين لا يعطي ترتيبًا جديدًا.

4 · Combinations — order does not matter

A selection of distinct objects without regard to order is called a combination.

Combination rule

nCr = n!(n − r)! · r!

Important: in a combination, the order or arrangement of the objects is not important; order is important in a permutation. Casio: to compute 10C4 → 10  SHIFT  ÷ (nCr)  4  =

الشرح بالعربي — التوافيق (الترتيب غير مهم)

nCr = عدد طرق اختيار r من n بدون ترتيب — مجرد اختيار.

علامات أن الترتيب غير مهم: لجنة بدون مناصب، فريق، مجموعة، اختيار كتب أو أفلام أو مواضيع، سحب عدة قطع من صندوق دفعة واحدة.

العلاقة: nCr = nPr ÷ r!، لذلك التوافيق دائمًا أصغر من أو تساوي التباديل.

مثال: اختيار 4 أفلام من 10 = 10C4 = 210. وإذا كان الاختيار من مجموعتين (3 نساء من 7 و رجلان من 5) فإننا نضرب: 7C3 × 5C2 = 35 × 10 = 350.

The relationship worth seeing once

nCr = nPr ÷ r! — a combination is a permutation with the r! internal orderings divided out. That is why nCr is always ≤ nPr. If your combination answer comes out larger than the matching permutation, you have used the wrong formula.

Example 4-48 · Movies at the Park

The director of Movies at the Park must select 4 movies from a total of 10 movies to show on Movie Night at the Park. How many different ways can the selections be made?

Just a selection — nothing says the four are shown in a particular order → combination.

10C4 = 10!(10 − 4)! · 4! = 10 · 9 · 8 · 74 · 3 · 2 · 1 = 5,04024 = 210 ways

Example 4-49 · Committee Selection

In a club there are 7 women and 5 men. A committee of 3 women and 2 men is to be chosen. How many different possibilities are there?

  1. Choose the women: 7C3 = 7!/(4!·3!) = (7·6·5)/(3·2·1) = 210/6 = 35
  2. Choose the men: 5C2 = 5!/(3!·2!) = (5·4)/2 = 10
  3. Combine with the Fundamental Counting Rule (a choice of women and a choice of men):
    35 × 10 = 350 possibilities
Trap — "and" means multiply, not add

Choosing 3 women and 2 men = 35 × 10 = 350. Students who write 35 + 10 = 45 are answering a different question ("choose 3 women or 2 men"). Within counting rules, and → ×, or → +.

6 · §4-5 — Probability and Counting Rules

This is the destination. The counting rules supply n(E) and n(S); classical probability divides them. The structure of every §4-5 question is identical:

  1. STEP 1Count the sample space. How many total ways can the selection be made? Usually a single nCr or a product.
  2. STEP 2Count the event. How many of those ways satisfy the condition? Usually a product of smaller C's — one per category.
  3. STEP 3Divide. P(E) = n(E) / n(S), then round sensibly and interpret in words.
الشرح بالعربي — الاحتمال باستخدام قواعد العدّ (أهم نوع أسئلة)

كل سؤال من هذا النوع له نفس الخطوات الثلاث:

  1. احسب n(S): عدد كل طرق الاختيار الممكنة — غالبًا nCr واحدة.
  2. احسب n(E): عدد الطرق التي تحقق الشرط — غالبًا حاصل ضرب توافيق، واحدة لكل فئة.
  3. اقسم: P(E) = n(E) ÷ n(S)، ثم اكتب جملة تفسيرية.

مثال: 24 قطعة منها 4 معيبة، نختار 4. احتمال قطعتين معيبتين بالضبط = (4C2 × 20C2) ÷ 24C4 = 1140 ÷ 10626 ≈ 0.1073. وإذا طُلب «واحدة معيبة على الأقل» ← 1 − P(none) = 1 − 0.4560 = 0.5440.

قاعدة الاتساق: استخدم نفس نوع العدّ في البسط والمقام — لا تضع تباديل فوق توافيق.

Example 4-51 · Defective Integrated Circuits

A box contains 24 integrated circuits, 4 of which are defective. If 4 are sold at random, find: (a) exactly 2 are defective; (b) none is defective; (c) all are defective; (d) at least 1 is defective.

Set-up. 4 defective, 20 good, 24 total. Choosing 4 with no order → combinations.

n(S) = 24C4 = 24 · 23 · 22 · 214 · 3 · 2 · 1 = 255,02424 = 10,626

  1. (a) Exactly 2 defective — so 2 from the 4 defective and 2 from the 20 good:
    n(E) = 4C2 × 20C2 = 6 × 190 = 1,140
    P = 1,14010,626 ≈ 0.1073
  2. (b) None defective — all 4 from the 20 good:
    n(E) = 20C4 = 20 · 19 · 18 · 1724 = 4,845
    P = 4,84510,626 ≈ 0.4560
  3. (c) All defective — all 4 from the 4 defective:
    n(E) = 4C4 = 1
    P = 110,626 ≈ 0.000094
  4. (d) At least 1 defective — the complement of "none defective", which is part (b):
    P = 1 − 0.4560 = 0.5440
Part (d) is the exam's reward for recognising the complement

Done the long way, (d) = P(1 defective) + P(2) + P(3) + P(4) — four calculations. Done as 1 − P(none), it is one subtraction you have already computed. This is precisely why parts (b) and (d) appear in the same question: the examiner is testing whether you noticed.

A student needs to select two topics to write two term papers for a course. There are 8 topics in economics and 11 topics in science. Find the probability that she selects one topic in economics and one topic in science.

  1. n(S): any 2 of the 19 available topics → 19C2 = 19 · 182 = 171
  2. n(E): one economics and one science → 8C1 × 11C1 = 8 × 11 = 88
  3. P = 88171 ≈ 0.5146

In the Pennsylvania State Lottery, a person selects a three-digit number and repetitions are permitted. If a winning number is selected, find the probability that it will have all three digits the same.

  1. n(S): three digits, repetition permitted → 10 × 10 × 10 = 1,000
  2. n(E): all three digits the same → 000, 111, 222, …, 999 → 10

Example 4-54 · Tennis Tournament

There are 8 married couples in a tennis club. If 1 man and 1 woman are selected at random to plan the summer tournament, find the probability that they are married to each other.

  1. n(S): 8 men to choose from and 8 women → 8 × 8 = 64 pairs
  2. n(E): only 8 of those pairs are actually married to each other (one per couple) → 8
  3. P = 864 = 18 = 0.125

HW 11 · Socks in a Drawer

A drawer contains 11 identical red socks and 8 identical black socks. Suppose you choose 2 socks at random in the dark. (a) What is the probability that you get a pair of red socks? (b) A pair of black socks? (c) 2 unmatched socks?

n(S): any 2 of 19 socks → 19C2 = 19 · 182 = 171

  1. (a) Pair of red: 11C2 = 11 · 102 = 55  →  P = 55171 ≈ 0.3216
  2. (b) Pair of black: 8C2 = 8 · 72 = 28  →  P = 28171 ≈ 0.1637
  3. (c) 2 unmatched: one red and one black: 11C1 × 8C1 = 88  →  P = 88171 ≈ 0.5146
    Or by complement: 1 − 0.3216 − 0.1637 = 0.5146 ✔

Check: 55 + 28 + 88 = 171 = n(S). The three cases are exhaustive and mutually exclusive, so their counts must total the sample space. ✔

Which rule do I use? كيف أختار القانون الصحيح خلال عشر ثوانٍ

Run this in order on every question. It takes ten seconds and it is the difference between a confident answer and a guess.

  1. Q1Does the question contain "not", "neither", "none", or "at least"?
    → Yes: use the complement, 1 − P(opposite). Compute the easy side. This check comes first because it can collapse the whole problem.
  2. Q2Does it say "given that", "if", "of those who", or state a fact as already settled?
    → Yes: conditional probability, P(B|A) = P(A∩B)/P(A). From a table, the condition's row or column total is the denominator.
  3. Q3Does it say "or"?
    → Addition rule. Then ask: can both happen together? No → P(A)+P(B). Yes → P(A)+P(B)−P(A∩B).
  4. Q4Does it say "and", or describe a sequence of stages?
    → Multiplication rule. Then ask: with replacement / separate devices? → independent, P(A)·P(B). Without replacement / "of those"? → dependent, P(A)·P(B|A).
  5. Q5Is the sample space too big to list?
    → Counting rules. Then ask: does order matter? Yes → nPr. No → nCr. Several categories combined → multiply them (Fundamental Counting Rule).
  6. Q6Is it asking for a probability after all that counting?
    → P(E) = n(E)/n(S). Count the event and the sample space with the same kind of rule — never mix a permutation numerator with a combination denominator.

  2. هل فيه given that / if / of those who، أو حقيقة وقعت مسبقًا؟ ← احتمال شرطي، والشرط في المقام.
  3. هل فيه or؟ ← قاعدة الجمع. هل يمكن وقوعهما معًا؟ لا ← اجمع. نعم ← اجمع واطرح التقاطع.
  4. هل فيه and أو مراحل متتالية؟ ← قاعدة الضرب. مع إرجاع ← اضرب مباشرة. بدون إرجاع ← الاحتمال الثاني يتغيّر.
  5. هل فضاء العينة أكبر من أن يُسرد؟ ← قواعد العدّ. الترتيب مهم ← nPr. غير مهم ← nCr. عدة فئات ← اضرب.
  6. هل المطلوب احتمال بعد العدّ؟ ← n(E) ÷ n(S)، بنفس نوع العدّ في البسط والمقام.
The one consistency rule that catches most §4-5 errors

Whatever you use for n(S), use for n(E). If the sample space counts unordered selections (combinations), the event must count unordered selections too. A numerator built from permutations over a denominator built from combinations is the most common wrong answer in the whole chapter, and it is always too large by a factor of r!.

The 14 traps that cost marks الأخطاء الأربعة عشر التي تضيّع الدرجات

Each of these has a specific home in this chapter. Read the list the night before the exam.

#The trapWhat to do instead
1Confusing mutually exclusive with independent.Mutually exclusive = no overlap (A∩B=∅). Independent = no influence (P(A∩B)=P(A)P(B)). Two different questions, two different tests.
2Using Addition Rule 1 when the events overlap.Always test the intersection first. When in doubt use Rule 2; it reduces to Rule 1 automatically.
3Reading "or" as exclusive.In probability, "A or B" always means A, or B, or both.
4Missing "without replacement".Circle it. It changes every denominator after the first draw and switches you from Rule 1 to Rule 2.
5Putting the wrong thing in the conditional denominator.The condition is the denominator. P(B|A) divides by P(A), never by P(B).
6Treating "of these clients…" / "of those who…" as unconditional.That phrasing always hands you a conditional probability. Annotate it as P(A|H) before you calculate.
7Mixing up "at most" and "less than" (and "at least" / "more than").at → the boundary value is included. than → the boundary value is excluded.
8Doing "at least one" the long way.1 − P(none). Always.
9Using a permutation for a committee.A committee has no internal order → combination. Labelled roles (chair, secretary) → permutation.
10Adding instead of multiplying across categories."3 women and 2 men" → 7C3 × 5C2. "And" is multiplication.
11Writing 0! = 0.0! = 1, by definition. It appears in nPn and nCn.
12Mixing permutations and combinations between n(E) and n(S).Count both with the same rule. Consistency, not cleverness.
13Giving only the final number.Write the sample space in set notation, name the rule you are using, show the substitution. The lecturer states explicitly that set notation is required.
14Reporting a probability greater than 1 or negative.0 ≤ P ≤ 1. If your answer falls outside, you have added when you should have multiplied, or forgotten to divide by n(S). Catch it before you move on.

الشرح بالعربي

هذه تمارين المراجعة الثلاثة في آخر ملف الدكتور. الفكرة المشتركة بينها: ارسم مخطط فن واملأ المناطق الأربع بالأرقام (A فقط، B فقط، المشترك، خارج الاثنين)، ثم يصبح كل فرع مجرد جمع لمناطق تراها أمامك. وتذكّر أن neither … nor تعني دائمًا 1 − P(A ∪ B).

Ex 1 · Burgers and pizza

A study shows that about 37 percent of school children eat burger, 17 percent eat pizza, and 10 percent eat both burger and pizza. What is the probability that a randomly selected school child eats neither burger nor pizza?

  1. Extract: P(B) = 0.37, P(Z) = 0.17, P(B ∩ Z) = 0.10.
  2. "Neither…nor" is the complement of the union. First get the union — they overlap, so Rule 2:
    P(B ∪ Z) = 0.37 + 0.17 − 0.10 = 0.44
  3. P(neither) = 1 − P(B ∪ Z) = 1 − 0.44 = 0.56

Cross-check with a Venn diagram: burger only = 0.37 − 0.10 = 0.27; pizza only = 0.17 − 0.10 = 0.07; both = 0.10; outside = 1 − (0.27 + 0.07 + 0.10) = 0.56. ✔

Ex 2 · Smart phone colors

If the probabilities are, respectively, 0.23, 0.15, 0.21, and 0.09 that a person purchasing a new smart phone will choose the color gold, silver, black, or white, what is the probability that a given buyer will purchase a new phone that comes in one of those colors?

  1. A phone has exactly one color, so the four events are mutually exclusive → extended Addition Rule 1.
  2. P(gold ∪ silver ∪ black ∪ white) = 0.23 + 0.15 + 0.21 + 0.09 = 0.68

Read what this tells you: the total is 0.68, not 1 — so 32% of buyers choose some other color entirely. A question that follows up with "find the probability the buyer chooses a different color" is answered by 1 − 0.68 = 0.32.

Ex 3 · Set algebra with probabilities

If P(A) = 0.3, P(B) = 0.2, and P(A ∩ B) = 0.1, determine: (a) P(Ac)  (b) P(A ∪ B)  (c) P(Ac ∩ B)  (d) P(A ∩ Bc)  (e) P((A ∪ B)c)  (f) P(Ac ∪ B)

Build the four-region picture first. A Venn diagram with two overlapping circles has exactly four regions, and every part of this question reads straight off it:

RegionMeaningProbability
A onlyA ∩ Bc0.3 − 0.1 = 0.2
BothA ∩ B0.1
B onlyAc ∩ B0.2 − 0.1 = 0.1
Neither(A ∪ B)c1 − 0.4 = 0.6
TotalS1.0 ✔
  1. (a) P(Ac) = 1 − P(A) = 1 − 0.3 = 0.7
  2. (b) P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B) = 0.3 + 0.2 − 0.1 = 0.4
  3. (c) P(Ac ∩ B) = "B but not A" = P(B) − P(A ∩ B) = 0.2 − 0.1 = 0.1
  4. (d) P(A ∩ Bc) = "A but not B" = P(A) − P(A ∩ B) = 0.3 − 0.1 = 0.2
  5. (e) P((A ∪ B)c) = 1 − P(A ∪ B) = 1 − 0.4 = 0.6
  6. (f) P(Ac ∪ B) = P(Ac) + P(B) − P(Ac ∩ B) = 0.7 + 0.2 − 0.1 = 0.8
    Or from the diagram: everything except "A only" = 1 − 0.2 = 0.8 ✔
The technique that makes this question trivial

Never attack set-algebra questions with formulas alone. Draw the two circles, fill in the four regions as numbers, and every sub-part becomes an addition of regions you can see. Parts (c), (d) and (f) take longer to write than to solve once the diagram exists.

The A+ study protocol بروتوكول الطالب الأول — استراتيجيات الوصول إلى A+

Chapter 4 rewards a specific kind of preparation: not more hours, but the right sequence. This is what the top student in a STAT 2101 section actually does, in order.

Phase 1 — Build the skeleton (90 minutes, once)

  1. 1.1Write the formula sheet by hand, from memory, with the page closed. Then open it and mark every gap in red. Repeat two days later. Recall is what the exam measures; re-reading is not recall and feels far more productive than it is.
  2. 1.2Make a one-page decision tree in your own words. Copy the "Which rule do I use?" section above, but phrase every branch yourself. The act of rewording is what moves it into memory.
  3. 1.3Learn the definitions as sentences, not as feelings. Sample space, outcome, event, simple, compound, mutually exclusive, independent, dependent, complement. These are CLO1 marks; they appear on quizzes verbatim and take one minute each to secure.

Phase 2 — Drill by question type, not by page order

  1. 2.1Group every exercise by which rule solves it before solving any of them. Fifteen minutes of sorting teaches you more about rule selection than two hours of grinding, because rule selection — not arithmetic — is where marks are lost.
  2. 2.2Do the assigned exercises properly: p. 247, numbers 6, 8, 16 and 40. These are named in the course outline, which means the tutor has them, which means variants of them are the most likely quiz questions in the course. Then do the Moodle exercises — they are listed for both Week 5 and Week 6.
  3. 2.3Solve every "Self-study" example in the slide deck. Examples 4-13, 4-21, 4-22, 4-26, 4-28, 4-40, 4-45, 4-54 and the three review exercises are marked self-study, which means they are not done in class and are therefore exactly where an examiner finds unfamiliar-looking material. All of them are solved in this document — close it and redo them from the question alone.
  4. 2.4Build the "twin" of each problem you solve. Did it say with replacement? Redo it without. Did it ask for "exactly 2"? Also do "at least 1". Twin-building is the single highest-yield habit in this chapter because the examiner builds twins for exactly the same reason.

Phase 3 — Write like a marker reads

  1. 3.1Open every answer by defining your events in symbols. "Let N = the staff person is a nurse, M = the staff person is male." Two lines, and the rest of the solution writes itself — and the marker can follow you even if the arithmetic slips.
  2. 3.2Write the sample space in set notation whenever it is small enough. The lecturer flags this explicitly on the Example 4-6 slide: "Sample spaces and events need to be expressed using set notations." That is a marking instruction in disguise.
  3. 3.3Name the rule before you use it. "Since the events are not mutually exclusive, Addition Rule 2 applies." One sentence, often one mark, and it forces you to check your own choice.
  4. 3.4Finish with a sentence in words. "So there is about a 54% chance that at least one circuit is defective." Interpretation is a stated outcome of this course (CLO8) and costs five seconds.

Phase 4 — The exam hour itself

  1. 4.1Brain-dump the formula sheet onto the back page in the first 90 seconds, before reading any question. You are then calculating from a written reference instead of from a memory under pressure.
  2. 4.2Underline the decisive words as you read: and, or, not, at least, at most, given, without replacement, order is important. Every one of them selects a formula. The question is telling you which rule to use; most lost marks come from not listening.
  3. 4.3Run the three checks on every numerical answer: is it between 0 and 1? do the complementary parts sum to 1? does the magnitude feel right (a rare event should come out small)? These catch nearly every slip.
  4. 4.4Budget by marks, not by difficulty. The midterm is 60 minutes across Chapters 1–4. Chapter 4 questions look longest but have the most structured method — do them with a timer at home so you know your real pace before exam night.
